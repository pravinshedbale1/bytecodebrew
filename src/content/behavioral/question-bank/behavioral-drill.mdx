Reading about behavioral answers doesn't make you good at them. Saying them out loud, against a clock, does. This drill gives you a random question from the bank of 152 (optionally from one theme), a two-minute timer, and a seven-point self-check. Your attempts are saved in this browser so you can watch your average climb.

## How to drill

1. **Pick a theme**, or leave it on "All themes" for interview-like randomness.
2. Read the question, take **up to 10 seconds** to choose a story, then **start the timer**.
3. **Answer out loud**, as if the interviewer were in the room. Recording yourself on your phone is even better.
4. **Score yourself honestly** against the checklist, then save the attempt.
5. Do **five a day**. Once your average is above 6 out of 7, switch to mock interviews with a friend who asks follow-ups.

## What the checklist measures

| Check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Opened with a one-line headline | The interviewer knows where you're going; you stay focused |
| Situation and task under 30 seconds | Context is the least valuable part of the answer |
| Actions numbered, each with a "because" | Depth, and evidence of judgment |
| Said "I" for my own actions | The interviewer can credit you |
| Result included a number | Evidence, not adjectives |
| Ended with what I learned | Self-awareness and growth |
| Finished in about two minutes | Respect for time; room for follow-ups |

These come straight from [The STAR Method, Done Right](/behavioral/foundations/star-method) and [Red Flags](/behavioral/playbook/red-flags).

<Callout type="tip" title="Make the drill harder as you improve">

- **Follow-ups**: after each answer, ask yourself "why?" three times and answer each.
- **Shorter version**: give the same story in 30 seconds.
- **Different angle**: retell a story so it answers a question from another theme.
- **A partner**: let a friend pick the questions and interrupt you.

</Callout>

<Takeaways>

- Practise out loud, against a clock: a random question, about two minutes, then an honest self-score.
- Five questions a day; your attempts and average are saved in this browser.
- When your average passes about 6 out of 7, move on to mock interviews with follow-up questions.
