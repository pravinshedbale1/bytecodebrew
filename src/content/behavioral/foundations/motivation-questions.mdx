"Why do you want to work here?" and "Why are you leaving your current job?" sound like small talk, but they're scored. The interviewer wants to know whether you'll be **engaged** (you want this job for real reasons), **stable** (you won't leave in six months) and **professional** (you don't trash your employer). Generic answers ("great culture", "growth") are the most common way to lose easy points here.

## Why this company?

A strong answer connects three things: **something specific about them**, **something true about you**, and **what you'd contribute**.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| "It's a great company with a great culture." | "You run payments at a scale where idempotency and reconciliation are hard problems, and that's exactly what I've worked on for three years." |
| "I want to grow." | "I want to move from owning one service to working on platform problems that many teams depend on. Your platform team's public talk on X is the kind of work I mean." |
| "Big brand." | "I've used the product daily for years. The part I'd love to work on is search: I've noticed Y and I'd be curious how you approach it." |

Research is how you get there:

- the product (use it);
- the engineering blog and public talks;
- recent launches, and the job description itself;
- the team, if you know which one.

*Cracking the Coding Interview* notes that Microsoft explicitly asks this question to test **passion for technology**. Apple interviewers expect you to know the product and have opinions on it, and a passion for the company comes through in your answers.

## Why are you leaving?

Answer **toward** something, not **away** from something. Give one honest reason, framed around what you want next, then stop.

| Avoid | Say instead |
| --- | --- |
| "My manager is terrible." | "I want a team with stronger engineering mentorship; I've learned what I can from my current setup." |
| "The pay is bad." | "I'm looking for a larger scope and responsibility, and I expect compensation to match." (Then leave money for the recruiter conversation.) |
| "I'm bored." | "I've built and stabilised the payments service; I'm looking for harder problems at a larger scale." |
| "They're laying people off and it's chaos." | "The company has changed direction, and the work I want to do isn't a priority there now." |

If you were laid off, say so plainly. It's common and not a judgment on you: "My role was part of a company-wide reduction in March." If you've moved jobs quickly several times, give a short reason for each and show what you're looking for in a longer-term home.

## Other motivation questions

- **"Where do you see yourself in five years?"** Show a direction, not a title: "Deeper in distributed systems, leading the design of larger pieces, and mentoring others. I'd like that to happen here."
- **"Why this role?"** Connect the job description to your experience and what you want to learn.
- **"What are you looking for in your next role?"** Two or three concrete things, which ideally this role offers.
- **"Do you have other offers?"** Be honest without details: "I'm in late stages with a couple of companies, but this team is my first choice because…" (only if true).

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes">

- **Generic praise** that could apply to any company.
- **Negativity** about current or past employers, colleagues or managers.
- **Only money or title** as the motivation.
- **Not knowing what the company or team does.**
- **Over-sharing**: one reason is enough.

</Callout>

<Practice task="Write a two-sentence answer to why this company for a company you are applying to. Underline the part that couldn't be said about any other company. If nothing is underlined, what research would fix it?">

Specific hooks include:

- a product feature you use and have a view on;
- a technical blog post or talk from their engineers;
- the scale or type of problem they have;
- a technology or approach they're known for;
- something in the job description that matches your experience.

Spend 30 minutes on their engineering blog and the job posting. One genuine, specific detail beats five compliments.

</Practice>

<Takeaways>

- Motivation answers are scored: they test engagement, stability and professionalism.
- Why this company = something specific about them, something true about you, and what you'd add.
- Why leaving = toward something, one honest reason, no negativity.
- Research the product, the engineering blog and the role so your answer couldn't be about any other company.

</Takeaways>
