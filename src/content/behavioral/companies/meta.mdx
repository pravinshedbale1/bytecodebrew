Meta's loop includes a dedicated behavioral interview. *Cracking the Coding Interview* (2015) records its internal nickname from the Facebook days: the **"Jedi"** interview, alongside "Ninja" for coding and "Pirate" for design. Meta's own prep article describes it as a conversation about your background and passions, to judge whether you'd **thrive in its fast-paced engineering organisation**. It's also a key input into the **level** you're offered, because interviewers look at the scope of what you've done.

## What Meta says about the behavioral interview

From Meta's article "Preparing for your software engineering interview at Meta":

- About **45 minutes**.
- It aims to learn your **background, what you're passionate about in tech, and the impact you want to make**, and whether you'd thrive in a fast-paced engineering organisation.
- **Know your resume**: expect questions about key events in your work history.
- **Be honest**: not every project succeeds and not every peer interaction goes smoothly. Being open about that "won't be counted against you", and what you learned is valued.
- **Use STAR**, with concrete examples rather than theory.
- **Be yourself, humble, and focus on teamwork, leadership and mentorship.**

The article lists these example themes:

- your best builds and what you're proud of;
- what you could have done better;
- past collaborations;
- pushing ideas despite opposition;
- handling conflict;
- giving and receiving feedback;
- technologies you're excited about.

## Meta's values

Meta's careers site lists six company values. Summaries in our own words:

| Value | What it means in an interview story |
| --- | --- |
| **Move Fast** | You shipped and learned quickly, removing blockers, with urgency |
| **Focus on Long-Term Impact** | You chose the durable fix or the bigger outcome over a quick win |
| **Build Awesome Things** | Quality and ambition: work you're proud of, not just "done" |
| **Live in the Future** | Early adoption; building with where things are going in mind |
| **Be Direct and Respect Your Colleagues** | Hard conversations held honestly and respectfully |
| **Meta, Metamates, Me** | Company and team before self; good stewardship |

## Scope decides your level

Interviewers listen for **how big your impact and ownership were**, because behavioral signal feeds the level decision. For the same question, stories at different levels sound like this:

| Level | Sounds like |
| --- | --- |
| Junior (E3-ish) | "I owned this feature and learned from a senior engineer's reviews." |
| Mid (E4-ish) | "I owned this project end to end and coordinated with design and one other team." |
| Senior (E5-ish) | "I led a multi-person project, set its technical direction and unblocked other teams." |
| Staff (E6+) | "I identified a problem across several teams and drove an organisation-level change." |

(Level names are approximate and widely reported by candidates, not taken from an official Meta source.) Choose stories at, or slightly above, the level you're targeting. See [Signals by Level](/behavioral/playbook/signals-by-level).

## Meta-specific tips

- **Prepare a conflict story with real tension.** Meta's article names handling conflict and pushing ideas despite opposition. A disagreement that was trivially resolved doesn't count for much.
- **Show speed with judgment**: how you moved fast **and** what you did to stay safe (flags, gradual rollouts, metrics).
- **Show growth**: feedback you acted on and how you changed. The article explicitly lists giving and receiving feedback.
- **Mention mentorship** if you have it; it's named in Meta's own advice.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes at Meta">

- Stories with a scope below the target level.
- No failure or "could have done better" story ready.
- Conflict stories with no real disagreement.
- Talking about technology without saying why it mattered to users or the team.

</Callout>

<Practice task="Take your strongest project story and tell it twice: once at your current level, once at the next level up. What would you have needed to do differently for the bigger version to be true?">

The next-level version usually adds:

- **more people affected** (other teams, not just yours);
- **more ambiguity** (you defined the problem, not just solved it);
- **more influence** (you set direction or changed a process).

If the bigger version isn't true yet, don't claim it. But the exercise shows which stories best signal the level you're targeting, and what to take on in your current job to grow into it.

</Practice>

<Takeaways>

- Meta's behavioral interview (about 45 minutes) explores your background, passions and impact, and whether you'll thrive in a fast-paced organisation.
- Meta's own advice: know your resume, be honest about failures, use STAR, give concrete examples, emphasise teamwork, leadership and mentorship.
- Its six values (Move Fast, Long-Term Impact, Build Awesome Things, Live in the Future, Be Direct, Meta Metamates Me) are good lenses for your stories.
- Scope matters because the behavioral round feeds the level decision.

</Takeaways>
