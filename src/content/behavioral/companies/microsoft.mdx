Microsoft's culture message under Satya Nadella has been the move from a "know-it-all" to a "learn-it-all" company, and it shows in interviews. Microsoft says it looks for candidates with a **growth mindset**: always learning, curious, willing to take risks, and quick to move on from mistakes. Its interviews are also more **team-driven** than most: different product groups run their loops differently, so it's worth asking your recruiter what to expect.

This page uses Microsoft's own careers culture page and a Microsoft news post on interview preparation. Teams vary, so confirm the format with your recruiter.

</Callout>

## What Microsoft says it looks for

Microsoft's careers site lists these culture attributes (summaries in our own words):

| Attribute | What it means in an interview story |
| --- | --- |
| **Growth mindset** | Potential is developed: curiosity, learning from mistakes, mentoring and being mentored |
| **Customer obsessed** | Understanding customers' real problems, building trust, solving with them |
| **Diverse and inclusive** | Seeking different perspectives; making sure everyone is heard and can contribute |
| **One Microsoft** | Working across team and organisational boundaries as one company |

The site also highlights **making a difference**: technology that earns trust, responsible AI, privacy, accessibility and sustainability.

A Microsoft news post on preparing for its interviews says it seeks candidates who show a growth mindset: always learning and curious, willing to lean into uncertainty and take risks, and moving quickly when they make mistakes. The post also recommends researching the role and company, showing genuine enthusiasm, and sending a thank-you note.

## How the loop works

- Typically a recruiter screen, one or more technical screens, then a loop of **four or five interviews** (*Cracking the Coding Interview* describes this, and current guides agree), often across one or two teams.
- **Team autonomy**: the book notes that experiences vary substantially across Microsoft, because different teams look for different things.
- **The "as appropriate" (AA) interview**: the book describes a final interview with a hiring manager or senior leader, reached if the loop has gone well. That description is from 2015, so ask your recruiter whether your loop includes a final hiring-manager conversation.
- Behavioral questions are woven into most interviews, not just one.

## Growth mindset in your stories

Interviewers listen for the **learning loop**: you tried, got something wrong or met something new, and changed.

- **Failure stories** that end with a concrete change ([Failure & Mistakes](/behavioral/question-bank/failure-and-mistakes)).
- **Feedback** you acted on ([Feedback & Growth](/behavioral/question-bank/feedback-and-growth)).
- **Learning something new fast**, with the method.
- **Changing your mind** when someone showed you better data.

Avoid know-it-all signals: dismissing other approaches, never having been wrong, or framing learning as something you've finished.

## "Why Microsoft?"

The book notes that Microsoft asks this to test **passion for technology**. Make it specific: a Microsoft product or platform you use and respect (Azure, VS Code, GitHub, .NET, TypeScript, Teams), what you'd want to work on, and why your experience fits that team.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes at Microsoft">

- Not asking which team or product group you're interviewing with. Preparation should differ.
- Know-it-all answers: never wrong, no learning.
- Generic "why Microsoft" answers.
- Ignoring customers, even for internal tools (the users are still customers).

</Callout>

<Practice task="Pick one story and retell it so the learning loop is unmistakable: what you believed at the start, what showed you otherwise, what you changed, and what you would teach someone else now.">

A growth-mindset retelling has four beats:

1. **Initial belief**: "I assumed staging data was representative."
2. **What challenged it**: an outage, feedback, data or a colleague.
3. **The change**: a new habit, tool or process.
4. **Paying it forward**: "I added it to our template and showed the team."

The fourth beat connects growth mindset to One Microsoft and mentoring. It also turns a personal lesson into team impact.

</Practice>

<Takeaways>

- Microsoft's culture attributes: growth mindset, customer obsessed, diverse and inclusive, One Microsoft.
- It says it seeks curious learners who lean into uncertainty and move quickly after mistakes.
- Loops vary by team; ask your recruiter which product group you're meeting and what to expect.
- Make the learning loop visible in your stories, and make "why Microsoft" specific.

</Takeaways>
