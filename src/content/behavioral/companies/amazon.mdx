Amazon runs the most structured behavioral interviews in big tech. Every interview, technical or not, is tied to its **Leadership Principles** (LPs), and interviewers are assigned principles to probe. Amazon's own interview guide says technical roles spend about **half the interview time on behavioral questions**. The guide also says the principles underpin hiring decisions, and that answers should be STAR stories with measurable results and lessons learned. If you interview at Amazon, preparing your stories against the LPs is not optional.

Principles and guidance on this page are taken from Amazon's own pages (linked at the end). Amazon updates its process and principles from time to time, so re-check the official list before your interview.

## How the loop works

- **Phone screen(s)**, then an **interview loop** of several interviews. Amazon says the process varies by role.
- **Each interviewer covers specific principles.** *Cracking the Coding Interview* notes that interviewers are each assigned a specific area to probe, so the interviews can feel very different from each other.
- **The Bar Raiser.** An experienced interviewer from **outside** the hiring team, whose job is to keep hiring standards high. The book notes they have significant interview experience and veto power, and that if one interview seems unusually hard and different, it's most likely the Bar Raiser.
- **Independent feedback.** The book says interviewers can't see each other's feedback until they've submitted their own, and then meet to decide.

## What Amazon says it wants in answers

From Amazon's own interview guide:

- **Behavioral questions** about real past situations, mapped to the Leadership Principles.
- **STAR structure**, about **2–3 minutes** per story, describing one specific situation.
- **Quantified impact**: data and metrics, especially for Customer Obsession and Bias for Action.
- **"I" statements**: what you personally did.
- **What you learned**, at the end of each story.

Expect **deep follow-up questions**. Candidates widely report that interviewers keep asking "why?" and "what exactly did you do?" until they reach the details. A shallow or borrowed story won't survive this.

## The 16 Leadership Principles, in interview terms

The descriptions below are summaries in our own words; read the full text on Amazon's site.

| Principle | What it means in an interview | Typical question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customer Obsession** | Start from the customer and work backwards; earn their trust | "Tell me about a time you went out of your way for a customer." |
| **Ownership** | Think long term; act for the whole company, not just your team; never "that's not my job" | "Tell me about a time you took on something outside your responsibilities." |
| **Invent and Simplify** | Find new, simpler ways; accept ideas from anywhere | "Tell me about a time you simplified a complex process." |
| **Are Right, A Lot** | Good judgment; seek diverse views; try to disprove your own beliefs | "Tell me about a decision you made with incomplete data." |
| **Learn and Be Curious** | Keep learning and exploring | "Tell me about something you learned on your own initiative." |
| **Hire and Develop the Best** | Raise the bar; grow other people | "Tell me about someone you mentored or developed." |
| **Insist on the Highest Standards** | Keep raising quality; fix problems so they stay fixed | "Tell me about a time you refused to compromise on quality." |
| **Think Big** | Bold direction; look for bigger ways to serve customers | "Tell me about a time you proposed something much more ambitious than asked." |
| **Bias for Action** | Speed matters; many decisions are reversible; take calculated risks | "Tell me about a time you acted without all the information." |
| **Frugality** | Do more with less; constraints breed resourcefulness | "Tell me about a time you delivered with limited resources." |
| **Earn Trust** | Listen; speak candidly; be self-critical, even when it's awkward | "Tell me about a time you admitted a mistake to your team." |
| **Dive Deep** | Stay close to the details; check when metrics and anecdotes disagree | "Tell me about a problem you solved by digging into the data." |
| **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit** | Challenge decisions respectfully; then commit fully once decided | "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager." |
| **Deliver Results** | Focus on the key inputs and deliver with quality, on time, despite setbacks | "Tell me about a time you delivered under a tight deadline." |
| **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer** | Build a safer, more inclusive, growth-oriented workplace | "Tell me about a time you improved your team's working environment." |
| **Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility** | Consider the wider impact of your decisions; leave things better | "Tell me about a time you considered the wider impact of a technical decision." |

## Map your story bank to the principles

You don't need sixteen stories. One story usually covers two or three principles. Here's how a set of eight stories might map:

| Story | Main principles it can answer |
| --- | --- |
| Duplicate-charge investigation | Dive Deep, Customer Obsession, Deliver Results |
| Postgres vs MongoDB disagreement | Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit, Are Right, A Lot |
| Flaky-tests clean-up | Ownership, Insist on the Highest Standards |
| UPI launch with cut scope | Deliver Results, Bias for Action, Frugality |
| Search outage I caused | Earn Trust, Learn and Be Curious |
| Mentoring a new graduate | Hire and Develop the Best |
| Contract tests across four teams | Think Big, Invent and Simplify |
| Checkout drop-off analysis | Dive Deep, Customer Obsession, Are Right, A Lot |

Use the [Story Bank](/behavioral/foundations/story-bank) and tag each story with the principles it can answer. Prepare **two stories** for the most frequently tested principles (Customer Obsession, Ownership, Deliver Results, Dive Deep, and Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit), so you don't repeat a story within a loop.

## Amazon-specific tips

- **Lead with data.** Numbers make "Deliver Results" and "Dive Deep" stories credible.
- **Show the customer** in every story you can, even internal customers (other teams).
- **Be ready to go five levels deep** on any detail: the queries you ran, who you spoke to, what you tried first.
- **Use your own failures** for Earn Trust; being self-critical is literally in the principle.
- **Read the job posting**: Amazon says postings often hint at which principles matter most for the role.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes at Amazon">

- Generic answers that don't match the principle being probed.
- "We" stories: the interviewer can't credit you for any principle.
- No metrics in Deliver Results or Dive Deep stories.
- Re-using the same story with every interviewer in the loop.
- Hypothetical answers ("I would…"). Amazon wants real examples.

</Callout>

<Practice task="Take three stories from your story bank and, for each, list which Leadership Principles it demonstrates. Which of the five most-tested principles has no story yet?">

Check coverage of the five most-tested principles: Customer Obsession, Ownership, Deliver Results, Dive Deep, and Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit. The usual gaps are:

- **Disagree and Commit**, if all your disagreements ended with you winning. Find one where you committed to a decision you disagreed with.
- **Customer Obsession**, if your work is internal. Reframe around the team or users who depended on it.

Then rehearse each story twice, emphasising a different principle each time.

</Practice>

<Takeaways>

- Amazon ties every interview to its 16 Leadership Principles; about half of a technical loop is behavioral.
- Answer with STAR in 2–3 minutes, "I" statements, metrics and lessons learned.
- A Bar Raiser from outside the team keeps the standard high; expect deep follow-ups.
- One story covers several principles; prepare two for the most-tested ones and don't repeat stories in a loop.

</Takeaways>
