Global banks and product companies run some of India's largest engineering centres, and their behavioral rounds reflect their industries. **Banks** weigh integrity, risk awareness and client service heavily; one careless answer about cutting corners can end an interview. **Enterprise software companies** weigh customer success and trust. All four below publish their values, so expect stories to be judged against them.

<Callout type="note" title="Checked October 2026">

Values are summarised in our own words from each company's own pages, except where marked. Re-check the official pages before your interview.

</Callout>

## Goldman Sachs

**Source: company page** (Goldman Sachs, "Purpose and values"). Four core values, which Goldman says are equally important:

| Value | Story to prepare |
| --- | --- |
| **Partnership**: collaboration, teamwork, the strength of the collective | Cross-team work where you shared credit |
| **Client Service**: a service mindset; earn trust, exceed expectations | A time you went beyond what an internal or external client asked |
| **Integrity**: the highest ethical standards, transparency, vigilance | A time you raised a concern or refused to cut a corner |
| **Excellence**: exceptional performance and superior results | A time you raised the quality bar and could measure it |

## JPMorgan Chase

**Source: company page** (JPMorgan Chase, "How We Do Business" business principles). The principles are organised under four headings:

| Principle | Story to prepare |
| --- | --- |
| **Exceptional client service** | Putting the customer's interest first, even against short-term gain |
| **Operational excellence** | Reliability, controls, process improvement, measured results |
| **A commitment to integrity, fairness and responsibility** | Doing the right thing; owning a mistake; handling sensitive data |
| **A great team and winning culture** | Collaboration, developing others, respectful directness |

## What banks listen for

Banks operate under heavy regulation, so the integrity signal matters more there than almost anywhere else:

- **Control and risk awareness.** Mention reviews, approvals, audit trails and safe rollouts naturally in your stories: "We released behind a flag, with a rollback plan approved by the change board."
- **Handling data.** Show care with personal and financial data: masking, access control, never copying production data casually.
- **Escalating properly.** In a conflict or a mistake story, show that you raised issues through the right channels, early.
- **Never a cutting-corners story told as a win.** "We skipped testing to hit the date" is a red flag anywhere and serious at a bank.

Prepare at least two stories from the **integrity** theme in the [question list](/behavioral/question-bank/all-questions).

## Salesforce

**Source: company page** (Salesforce, "Our story"). Five core values, with **Trust** first:

| Value | Story to prepare |
| --- | --- |
| **Trust**: ethical leadership and reliable technology | Reliability, security or privacy work; owning an incident openly |
| **Customer Success**: help customers reach their goals | A feature or fix measured by what it did for customers |
| **Innovation**: products that are easy to use and quick to deliver value | A simplification or a new capability |
| **Equality**: inclusion and fairness | Making sure everyone on the team could contribute |
| **Sustainability**: environmental responsibility | Efficiency work, such as reducing compute cost or waste, if you have it |

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes at these companies">

- A story where speed beat safety, told proudly. At a bank, this is a serious red flag.
- Vague "client focus" with no client: name who benefited, even an internal team.
- Treating values questions as a formality. Adobe, for one, says its values guide how it hires.
- Not knowing what the India centre builds (trading platforms, payments, Creative Cloud, Customer 360…). Research the specific team.

</Callout>

<Practice task="Write an integrity story: a time you raised a concern, refused a shortcut, or handled sensitive data carefully. Then check: does it show WHEN you raised it, WHO you told, and what happened as a result?">

A strong integrity story has:

- **an early signal**: "I noticed on Monday that…";
- **the right channel**: your manager, the owner of the system, or a security or risk contact, not a public complaint;
- **a respectful framing**: the risk, not blame;
- **a resolution**: the shortcut wasn't taken, or was taken with explicit sign-off and safeguards.

If your story ends with "and nobody listened", add what you did next, and what you'd do differently.

</Practice>

<Takeaways>

- Goldman Sachs: partnership, client service, integrity, excellence. JPMorgan: client service, operational excellence, integrity, team.
- Adobe: genuine, exceptional, innovative, involved. Salesforce: trust first, then customer success, innovation, equality and sustainability.
- Banks weigh integrity, risk awareness and escalation heavily; never present a corner cut as a win.
- Prepare two integrity stories, and research the specific India-centre team.

</Takeaways>
