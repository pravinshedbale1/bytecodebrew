India's product companies interview differently from the classic big-tech loop, but they're just as values-driven. Several publish their values and say they hire against them. Razorpay, for example, says its interview process is "deeply intertwined" with its six values. The typical loop for engineers is coding, then design (low-level and/or high-level), then a **hiring-manager or culture round**. That last round is where your stories, your project deep dive and your motivation are tested. This lesson collects what each company says it values, and turns it into stories to prepare.

<Callout type="note" title="Checked October 2026 — and how sure we are">

Each section says where its values come from:

- **Company page**: the company's own careers page or blog.
- **Job postings**: values text used in the company's own job listings.
- **Press reports**: interviews and articles about the company, where no official list was found.

Values change, so check the official page before your interview.

</Callout>

## The loop at Indian product companies

Candidates commonly report a structure like this. Formats vary by company, team and level, so confirm with your recruiter.

| Round | What it tests | Where your behavioral prep helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Online assessment / coding | DSA | — |
| Machine coding / LLD | Clean, extensible code under time pressure | Explaining your design choices |
| HLD / system design (mid–senior) | Architecture and trade-offs | The project deep dive |
| **Hiring manager / culture fit** | Ownership, impact, collaboration, motivation, level | Stories, pitch, why us, why leave |

The hiring-manager round often decides the **level**, and it often probes **why you're switching** and **notice period**. See [Notice Periods, Counter-offers & Resigning](/behavioral/playbook/notice-periods-and-counteroffers).

## Flipkart

**Source: company page** (Flipkart's early-careers site; the same five values appear in Flipkart's own stories blog and job postings).

| Value | Story to prepare |
| --- | --- |
| **Audacity**: think big, take bold bets, change the status quo | A time you proposed something much more ambitious than asked |
| **Bias for Action**: move fast and purposefully on problems | A time you acted quickly with incomplete information |
| **Customer First**: see the world from the customer's side | A change you made because of what customers experienced |
| **Integrity**: do what's right, and do what you said you'd do | A time you kept a commitment at a cost, or raised an uncomfortable truth |
| **Inclusion**: value differences; make everyone belong | A time you made sure a quieter or newer colleague was heard |

Flipkart's own blog describes awards tied to these values. That's a hint that interviewers look for the same behaviours.

## Swiggy

**Source: company page** (Swiggy's blog, "What We Value at Swiggy", December 2022). Swiggy groups its eleven values into three parts:

| Group | Values |
| --- | --- |
| **Heart: who we are** | Be Humble · Always be Curious, Always be Learning · Be Honest, Display Highest Level of Integrity |
| **Head: how we think** | Consumer Comes First · Display a Founder Mentality · Think Win-Win · Think Big |
| **Hands: how we act** | Stand-up & Disagree but Commit Fully · Do More with Less · Move Fast, Break Barriers and Deliver Results · Never Settle |

What to prepare:

- **Win-win** is distinctive. Swiggy's marketplace has consumers, restaurant partners and delivery partners. Prepare a story where you balanced the needs of more than one group.
- **Disagree but commit fully** closely mirrors Amazon's principle. Have a disagree-and-commit story ready ([Conflict](/behavioral/question-bank/conflict-and-disagreement)).
- **Founder mentality** and **do more with less** call for ownership stories with scrappy, resourceful solutions ([Ownership](/behavioral/question-bank/ownership-and-ambiguity)).

## Razorpay

**Source: company page** (Razorpay blog, "Beyond resumes: setting A-teams through values-driven interviews", April 2024). It names six values and says what candidates should be ready to discuss for each:

| Value | Razorpay suggests being ready to discuss… |
| --- | --- |
| **Customer First**: "What's in it for our customers?" | Times you put customer needs first and improved their experience |
| **Autonomy & Ownership**: act independently, own goals | Projects where you wore several hats, cleared blockers or worked without clear instructions |
| **Challenging the Status Quo** | Improvements you proposed, and what happened |
| **Agility with Integrity**: move fast, ethically | Adapting to change, missed deadlines, decisions that didn't work out |
| **Transparency**: share widely, raise risks early | When poor information-sharing hurt a team, and what you did |
| **Razorpay Grows with Razors**: individual growth | Your career plan and how Razorpay fits it |

This is unusually explicit guidance. Map one story to each row before a Razorpay interview, and prepare a **failure** story for "Agility with Integrity".

## PhonePe

**Source: job postings.** PhonePe's careers page doesn't list values. Its own job postings have listed values including:

- Integrity
- Positive Disruption
- Learn Fast, Fail Fast
- Bias for Impact
- Transparency
- Collaboration
- Excellence in People
- Openness of Thought
- Customer First
- Holistic Approach

A 2022 article by a PhonePe engineering leader (YourStory) describes the engineering culture as **high ownership, comfort with ambiguity, continuous learning and leading through positive influence**.

What to prepare:

- Stories with **scale and reliability** (payments at very high volume);
- **learning from a failure quickly** (Learn Fast, Fail Fast);
- **influence without authority**;
- a **bias for impact**: a result you can quantify.

## Zomato / Eternal

**Source: press reports.** The parent company was renamed from Zomato to Eternal. We didn't find an official, current values list for engineering hiring. Press coverage (People Matters) describes traits the company looks for:

- a **founder's mindset**;
- a **people-centric approach**;
- a **spirit of service and innovation**;
- a culture that treats **failure as learning**;
- **transparent, non-anonymous feedback**.

Prepare ownership, failure-and-learning and candid-feedback stories, and check the company's careers page for its current wording before interviewing.

## Common threads, and how to prepare

Across these companies, five behaviours come up again and again:

| Behaviour | Companies naming it | Your story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Customer / consumer first** | Flipkart, Swiggy, Razorpay, PhonePe | A change driven by what users experienced |
| **Ownership / founder mentality** | Swiggy, Razorpay, PhonePe, Zomato (reported) | A problem nobody owned that you drove to the end |
| **Speed / bias for action** | Flipkart, Swiggy, Razorpay, PhonePe | Fast delivery with sensible safeguards |
| **Integrity / transparency** | Flipkart, Swiggy, Razorpay, PhonePe | Raising a risk early; keeping a commitment |
| **Disagree and commit / challenge status quo** | Swiggy, Razorpay | A respectful disagreement, then full commitment |

<Callout type="tip" title="Questions you'll almost certainly get">

- "Why are you leaving your current company?" ([Motivation](/behavioral/foundations/motivation-questions))
- "What's your notice period? Can you join earlier?" ([Notice periods](/behavioral/playbook/notice-periods-and-counteroffers))
- "Walk me through your current project's architecture." ([Project Deep Dive](/behavioral/foundations/project-deep-dive))
- "What's the biggest impact you've had?" ([Impact](/behavioral/question-bank/impact-and-customers))

</Callout>

<Practice task="Pick one company on this page. For each of its values, write the title of the story from your bank that shows it. Which value is hardest to cover, and what story could fill it?">

The usual gaps:

- **Win-win** (Swiggy): look for a time you balanced two groups, such as two teams, a customer and the business, or partners and users.
- **Transparency** (Razorpay, PhonePe): a time you shared bad news or a risk early.
- **Audacity / Think Big** (Flipkart, Swiggy): a time you proposed a much bigger change than asked.

Write one in STAR form and tag it in your [story bank](/behavioral/foundations/story-bank).

</Practice>

<Takeaways>

- Indian product companies typically combine coding, design and a hiring-manager or culture round, where stories, motivation and level are decided.
- Flipkart: five values. Swiggy: eleven values in Heart, Head and Hands. Razorpay: six values with explicit interview guidance.
- PhonePe's values come from its job postings, and Zomato/Eternal's traits from press reports. Check their current pages.
- Common threads: customer first, ownership, speed, integrity and transparency, and disagree-and-commit.

</Takeaways>
