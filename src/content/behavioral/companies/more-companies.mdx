Many companies publish their values and expect candidates to have read them. Interviewers often score behavioral answers against exactly those values. This lesson covers five companies with distinctive cultures, and gives a method that works for **any** company, including startups and product companies that don't publish much.

<Callout type="note" title="Checked October 2026">

Values below are summarised in our own words from each company's own pages (linked under each section). They change occasionally, so re-check before your interview.

</Callout>

## The method for any company

1. **Find their values**: the careers or culture page, the engineering blog, the founders' letters.
2. **Translate each value into a behaviour** you could show in a story.
3. **Tag your story bank** with those values (the [Story Bank](/behavioral/foundations/story-bank) has free-form titles; add the value names to them).
4. **Prepare one strong story per value** they emphasise most, and use their vocabulary naturally, without parroting.

## Netflix

Netflix's culture memo is one of the most famous in tech. Its current version lists eight values for its "dream team":

| Value | In an interview story |
| --- | --- |
| **Selflessness** | You put the company's interest first and helped colleagues grow |
| **Judgment** | Long-term solutions; good calls under uncertainty, using data and instinct |
| **Candor** | You gave and asked for honest feedback; you shared your mistakes openly |
| **Creativity** | New ideas and unconventional solutions |
| **Courage** | You took a risk or challenged the status quo for a better result |
| **Inclusion** | You recognised your own biases and helped everyone do their best work |
| **Curiosity** | You learned fast and valued others' ideas; you admitted what you didn't know |
| **Resilience** | You adapted to change, decided without long delays, took on hard problems |

Two ideas in the memo shape the interviews:

- **"People over process"**, with leaders giving **context, not control**. Expect questions about decisions you made yourself with the context you had.
- **The keeper test**: managers ask whether they'd fight to keep someone. Netflix hires for high performance and expects **extraordinary candour**. Prepare stories about giving difficult feedback, including to more senior people.

Source: [Netflix Culture Memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture).

## Atlassian

Atlassian has had the same five values since 2006, and it says they shape its culture and even who it hires, so expect questions aimed at them.

| Value | In an interview story |
| --- | --- |
| **Open company, no bullshit** | Transparency: you shared information and bad news openly and early |
| **Build with heart and balance** | Passion for the work, balanced with sustainable pace and good judgment |
| **Don't #@!% the customer** | You protected customers, even when it cost you time or effort |
| **Play, as a team** | Collaboration and team success over individual credit |
| **Be the change you seek** | You improved something yourself instead of waiting for someone else |

Source: [Atlassian core values](https://www.atlassian.com/company/values).

## Stripe

Stripe publishes six operating principles describing how it makes decisions, collaborates and serves users:

| Principle | In an interview story |
| --- | --- |
| **Users first** | You worked backwards from what users needed, especially the most demanding users |
| **Create with craft and beauty** | You cared about quality and polish, not just "working" |
| **Move with urgency and focus** | You moved fast on what mattered most, and invested in making the team faster |
| **Collaborate egolessly** | You shared information, challenged ideas openly, gave credit, learned from mistakes |
| **Stay curious** | You dug into how something works before deciding |
| **Obsess over talent** | You helped hire or develop strong people and kept the bar high |

Stripe's culture is often described as writing-heavy, so clear design docs and written reasoning are good evidence for several of these.

Source: [How Stripe works](https://stripe.com/jobs/culture).

## Uber

Uber introduced its current cultural values in 2021. As listed on Uber's own pages, they are:

- Go get it
- Trip obsessed
- Build with heart
- Stand for safety
- Great minds don't think alike
- Do the right thing
- See the forest and the trees
- One Uber

Uber describes **Go get it** as bringing a champion's mindset and ambition. **Trip obsessed** is about the trip being where the marketplace comes to life. **Build with heart** means putting yourself in the shoes of the people who use the marketplace. **Stand for safety** means safety is built into everything. For interviews, prepare:

- stories about **user empathy across both sides of a marketplace** (riders and drivers, eaters and couriers);
- a story about **safety or reliability** that you refused to compromise;
- a story about a **diverse view that changed your mind** ("Great minds don't think alike").

Source: Uber's careers values page and its 2023 "Reimagine Awards" post on uber.com, which recognised employees for each of the eight values.

## Apple

Apple doesn't publish an interview values framework like the companies above, so rely on what's consistently reported. *Cracking the Coding Interview* (2015) describes the Apple process as low on bureaucracy and team-based: you interview with the team and people it works with, sometimes in two-on-one interviews. Interviewers look for excellent technical skill and **real passion for the product and the company**. Its advice still holds:

- **Know the product** your team builds; use it.
- Have **specific opinions**: what you like, and what you'd improve.
- Show **care for detail and quality** in your stories.
- Expect questions to go **deep into your technical area**, because the interviews are team-specific.

## Startups and product companies

Companies without published values tend to probe the same few things:

| Signal | Why it matters there | Story to prepare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ownership** | Small teams, no one to hand off to | Something you drove end to end |
| **Speed with judgment** | Shipping fast without breaking trust | A fast launch with safeguards |
| **Ambiguity** | Specs are thin or missing | Turning a vague goal into a plan |
| **Breadth** | You'll touch many areas | Learning a new area quickly |
| **Customer contact** | Engineers often talk to users | A change driven by user feedback |

Read the founders' posts and the job description, and ask in the interview which of these matters most to them.

<Practice task="Pick one company from this page you might apply to. For each of its values, write which story from your bank shows it. Which value has no story, and which past situation could become one?">

Values that often lack a story:

- **Candour / Open company**: find a time you delivered bad news early, or gave hard feedback.
- **Courage / Be the change**: a time you challenged a decision or fixed something nobody owned.
- **Inclusion**: a time you made sure a quieter or newer colleague was heard, or changed a practice so it worked for everyone.

These situations are common at work; people just don't think of them as stories. Write one in STAR form and add it to your bank.

</Practice>

<Takeaways>

- Find a company's values, translate each into behaviour, and tag your stories with them.
- Netflix: eight values, people over process, extraordinary candour and the keeper test.
- Atlassian: five long-standing values, used in hiring. Stripe: six operating principles, writing-heavy culture.
- Uber: eight values centred on the marketplace, safety and ambition. Apple: product passion, detail and team-specific depth.
- Startups test ownership, speed with judgment, ambiguity, breadth and closeness to customers.

</Takeaways>
