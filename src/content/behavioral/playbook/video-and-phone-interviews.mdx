Most first rounds, and many full loops, now happen over video or phone. The content of your answers doesn't change, but the medium removes the cues a room gives you: eye contact, the interviewer's body language, the ability to sketch on a whiteboard. Small preparation removes most of the risk, and lets your stories do the work.

## Set-up checklist

- [ ] **Stable connection.** Wired if possible; a phone hotspot ready as a backup.
- [ ] **Camera at eye level**, face lit from the front (a window or lamp behind the screen, not behind you).
- [ ] **Headphones with a microphone.** They cut echo and background noise.
- [ ] **Quiet room**, notifications off on every device, and the household warned.
- [ ] **Test the exact platform** (Zoom, Teams, Meet, Chime) the day before; log in 5 minutes early.
- [ ] **Have the recruiter's phone number** in case the call drops.
- [ ] **A glass of water.**

## Presence on video

- **Look at the camera when you make your key points**, not at the interviewer's face on screen. To them, that's eye contact.
- **Pause slightly longer** than in person before answering. Audio lag makes people talk over each other.
- **Signal that you're thinking**: "Let me take a second to pick the best example." Silence on video feels longer than it is.
- **Check in** after a long answer: "Would you like more detail on the migration, or shall I move on?" You can't read their body language as easily.
- **Keep your story grid nearby, not on the screen you're looking at.** Glancing at keywords is fine; reading a script is obvious on camera.

## Phone screens

Without video, your voice carries everything:

- **Stand up or sit up straight**: it makes your voice more energetic.
- **Smile when you greet them.** It's audible.
- **Signpost more than usual**: "I'll answer in three parts", "the result was…".
- **Keep answers slightly shorter**; it's harder to hold attention by voice alone.

## Online collaboration for design and deep dives

When you need to sketch an architecture or a timeline:

- Ask at the start which tool to use, and practise it (Excalidraw, Miro, a Google Doc, the platform's whiteboard).
- **Narrate while you draw**: "I'll put the API gateway here, and the order service behind it."
- Keep the drawing simple: boxes, arrows and labels.

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistakes">

- Being the person with the unstable connection and no backup plan.
- Reading answers from a second screen; your eyes give it away.
- Talking for five minutes without checking in.
- A cluttered or noisy background that pulls attention from your answers.

</Callout>

<Takeaways>

- Test the platform, the connection, the camera and the sound before the day, and have a backup.
- Look at the camera for key points, pause for lag, and check in after long answers.
- On the phone, signpost clearly and keep answers a little shorter.
- Narrate while you draw, in a tool you've practised.

</Takeaways>
