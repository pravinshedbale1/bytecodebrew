"Where do I start, and what do I do next?" is the question that stalls most people for weeks. This plan answers it: ten weeks, about **8–10 hours a week**, **112 carefully chosen problems** (plus 6 optional stretch problems), in an order where each week builds on the last. Every problem links to LeetCode and has a checkbox that feeds the [Problem Tracker](/dsa/playbook/problem-tracker). It uses the [three-pass method](/dsa/start-here/how-to-practice) — attempt, study, re-solve — and leaves room for revision and mock interviews, because those are what turn practice into offers.

New to data structures? Add a **week 0**: read [Data Structures Basics](/dsa/data-structures/arrays-and-strings) (eight short lessons with visuals) before week 1. Otherwise, dip into a basics lesson whenever a pattern lesson's "New to…?" note points you there.

Short on time? The **4-week fast track** at the end keeps the highest-value problems.

## How each week works

| Day | What to do | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Read the week's lessons; write each template from memory once | 1.5–2 h |
| Days 2–4 | New problems from the week's list (attempt → study → write it yourself) | 1.5–2 h each |
| Day 5 | **Re-solve** last week's hardest 3–4 problems from a blank page | 1–1.5 h |
| Day 6 | One **mixed** problem from any earlier week, timed (40 min), out loud | 1 h |
| Day 7 | Rest, or catch up | — |

Log every problem with its **pattern and signal** ("sliding window — longest contiguous + condition"). Before each new week, spend 10 minutes reading the log's signal column.

## Signs you're ready

- You can write every template in the [Cheat Sheet](/dsa/playbook/dsa-cheat-sheet) from memory.
- On an unseen medium problem, you name a plausible pattern within two or three minutes.
- You solve most mediums in 25–35 minutes, out loud, with a correct complexity statement.
- You re-solve old problems quickly, and your mistakes are slips, not "no idea".

## Interview questions

<QA q="How many problems should I solve before interviewing?">
There's no magic number. Around 120–150 well-chosen problems, each solved with the attempt–study–re-solve routine and logged with its pattern and signal, prepares most people for typical interviews. What matters more is coverage of every major pattern and the ability to recognise them in unseen problems, which comes from mixed practice and revision rather than from volume alone.
</QA>

<Practice task="You have 6 weeks and about 6 hours a week. Adapt the plan: what do you merge, and what do you keep no matter what?">

Merge pairs of weeks with related topics (weeks 5+6 and 7+8 work well), and take only the first 6–7 problems of each list, keeping at least one Hard per heavy pattern. **Never drop**: the re-solve day, logging the pattern and signal, and at least two timed out-loud mocks in the final week. Those three habits are what make the smaller number of problems stick.

</Practice>

<Takeaways>

- Ten weeks, 112 problems, in an order where each topic builds on the last.
- Every week: lessons, new problems, a re-solve day and one timed mixed problem.
- Log the pattern and signal for every problem; revise the signals before each week.
- Week 10 is for mixed practice, spotting drills and mock interviews.
- Short on time: the 4-week fast track keeps the highest-value 40 problems.

</Takeaways>
